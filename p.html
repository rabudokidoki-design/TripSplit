Why This Matters

What's At Stake: Your Research Data Is the Company

Your research data is patentable, licensable and valuable for 10–20+ years. Most of it sits in shared folders, lab notebooks and chat apps across two jurisdictions, with no one formally responsible for protecting it.

Patent priority undermined

Unpublished results leaked or disclosed early — through an over-shared link or a departing researcher — can destroy novelty or allow a competitor to file first. Consequence: years of R&D with no enforceable protection.

Long-tail value, single copies

Raw data generated today supports filings, licensing deals and regulatory submissions a decade from now. Much of it exists in one place with no tested backup. Consequence: ransomware or accidental deletion erases the asset permanently.

Insider and collaboration risk

Researchers move between institutions and share freely with external partners. Without leaver controls and classification rules, data walks out the door. Consequence: the most likely IP-loss event is quiet, not a headline breach.

Cross-border exposure

Data moves daily between Hong Kong and the Mainland over sync tools, email and messaging apps. Biotech IP is a known target for state-aligned and commercial espionage. Two regulatory regimes apply. Consequence: loss of control over where data is, and regulatory exposure nobody has mapped.

Investor and partner expectations

IPO investors, licensing partners and the sponsor's due diligence will ask how research data integrity is protected. "We trust our people" is not an answer. Consequence: findings late in the IPO process cost more to fix and are harder to explain.

Our position

This is asset-protection work. The IPO is the forcing deadline, not the reason it matters. Gate 1 builds a defensible minimum baseline in six months. It does not promise certification or perfection — it establishes ownership, visibility and the controls that address the most realistic ways research data is lost.

Governing Frame & Methodology

Control catalogue: ISO/IEC 27001:2022 + 27002:2022

ISO 27001 is the international standard for information security management. Its companion, ISO 27002, lists 93 controls in four themes: organisational, people, physical and technological. We use it to define what "good" looks like. We do not pursue certification in Gate 1.

ISO alone tells you what to build but not how much is enough today. A maturity model alone tells you how far along you are but not against what. Together they let us say: "Here is the control, here is where you are, here is the realistic Gate 1 target, here is the long-term goal."

Context · Orientation Only

HK IPO Regulatory Context: Two Data Regimes, One Listing Process

HKEX listing process — where this work lands

  • Chapter 18A (pre-revenue biotech) — the sponsor's due diligence will examine how core R&D assets are protected and governed. Confirm listing route in Week 1.
  • Internal control review — the sponsor typically commissions an IC consultant whose IT general controls (ITGC) testing covers access, change and backup. Our evidence pack is designed to be reusable by them; we do not duplicate their financial-controls work.
  • Corporate Governance Code — post-listing, the board oversees risk management and internal control and reviews effectiveness at least annually. Gate 1 produces a one-page board briefing to start that habit.
  • Post-listing disclosure — a material cyber incident may constitute inside information requiring disclosure. Incident readiness therefore has a listed-company dimension.

Hong Kong — Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance (PDPO)

  • Six Data Protection Principles govern collection, retention, use, security, transparency and access. They apply to staff and any research-subject personal data held in HK.
  • Section 33 (cross-border transfer restriction) was enacted but has not been brought into operation. The DPPs still apply to transferred data.
  • The PCPD's cross-border guidance and Recommended Model Contractual Clauses (2022) represent expected good practice for transfers, including intra-group HK ⇄ Mainland.

Hong Kong's Protection of Critical Infrastructures (Computer Systems) Ordinance is not expected to apply — the Company is not a designated operator. Confirm Week 1.

Mainland — three laws plus a biotech-specific regime

  • Cybersecurity Law (2017), Data Security Law (2021), Personal Information Protection Law (2021) — impose conditions on personal information and "important data" leaving China (security assessment, standard contract or certification, depending on volume and sensitivity).
  • CAC Provisions on Promoting and Regulating Cross-border Data Flows (March 2024) — raised volume thresholds and allow organisations to presume data is not "important data" unless a regulator has notified them or published a catalogue.
  • Human Genetic Resources (HGR) Regulations and 2023 Implementing Rules (MOST) — restrict providing or opening access to human gene/genome data to foreign entities (including HK-incorporated ones); security review is required above defined thresholds (e.g. exome/genome sequencing data of 500+ individuals). If your research includes human genetic or genomic data generated in the Mainland, this is the single most important cross-border item to confirm in Week 1.

Scope Rule

The Backup Rule: One Statement, Applied Consistently

Backup is where "configure existing features" and "deploy new tools" meet. To avoid ambiguity, one rule governs backup in Tool Strategy (pages 14–15), New Tool Categories (pages 18–19) and Scope Dimension 4 (page 23).

Included in the Gate 1 fee, regardless of door

1. Native protection configured — versioning and retention on OneDrive research folders and, where supported, Jianguoyun. Recycle-bin and retention periods set to the maximum the licence allows.

2. Backup requirement written — what is backed up, how often, how long retained, where stored (separate from the production platform and its credentials), and the immutability expectation (cannot be altered or deleted by a compromised admin account for a defined period).

3. One restore test — a documented recovery of sample research folders from native version history, timed and signed off, producing the Backup/Restore Test Report.

Recommended · Door A
Client procures a lightweight SaaS backup licence during Gate 1; we configure it.
  • Independent third-party backup of OneDrive (and Jianguoyun if an integration exists) for Restricted-Research folders
  • Stored outside the production tenant, with immutability/retention lock
  • Our effort included: ~2–3 consultant-days for configuration and a second restore test from the independent copy
  • Excluded: licence cost (typically HK$30–80/user/year for M365 backup SaaS — indicative; vendor selection remains out of scope, we provide the requirement and three suitable category examples)

Addresses: ransomware that also encrypts or deletes version history; admin-account compromise; accidental bulk deletion.

Alternative · Door B
Defer independent backup procurement to Gate 2; rely on native versioning until then.
  • Lower cash cost in Gate 1; no procurement during the IPO window
  • Native versioning protects against most accidental deletion and many ransomware variants
  • Interim gap: an attacker or rogue admin with tenant-level access can purge versions and retention; sync-based platforms may not protect against all variants
  • Requires Sponsor sign-off on the Residual Risk Acceptance list

Consequence of inaction: the one scenario this leaves open is total, permanent loss of research data — the highest-impact event in this proposal.

Cross-Border Data Transfer · Orientation

Why Two Regimes Apply — and Why This Is Its Own Risk Category

Research, personal and financial data move between Hong Kong and Shanghai, Beijing and Wuxi every day. Hong Kong and the Mainland are separate jurisdictions for data law. A transfer that is routine to your staff is a regulated event in at least one direction — and the research-data security risk exists regardless of the law.

Hong Kong side — PDPO

  • Applies to personal data held or controlled in HK (staff records, any research-subject data).
  • Section 33 (transfer restriction) is enacted but not in operation. The Data Protection Principles still apply to data sent to the Mainland — in particular DPP 4 (security) and DPP 3 (use).
  • PCPD guidance and Recommended Model Contractual Clauses (2022) set out expected practice for intra-group transfers. Counsel can advise whether to adopt them for HK ⇄ Mainland flows.

Mainland side — CSL / DSL / PIPL / HGR rules

  • Personal information leaving China: route depends on volume and sensitivity (exempt below thresholds; standard contract; or CAC security assessment). The March 2024 CAC Provisions relaxed thresholds for many ordinary businesses.
  • "Important data": data whose compromise could harm national security, the economy or public interest. Under the 2024 Provisions you may presume data is not important data unless notified or catalogued — but sectoral catalogues are evolving, and biotech is a watched sector.
  • Human genetic resources information: a distinct regime. Providing or opening access to Mainland human gene/genome data to a foreign entity — which can include an HK-incorporated parent — requires filing or security review above thresholds. This is the item most likely to be material for a biotech.

Why it is its own risk category here

  • It is distinct from platform configuration: a perfectly configured OneDrive still receives Mainland files via WeChat screenshots.
  • It is distinct from regulatory awareness: the security risk (interception, uncontrolled copies, no logs) exists even where the law permits the transfer.
  • Nobody has mapped the flows. Until they are mapped, neither we nor counsel can say which rules bite.

What this section is

Operational awareness and a factual flow map, so that controls can be applied and counsel can be briefed efficiently. It is not a legal compliance opinion, not legal advice, and does not include any PRC regulatory filing. Items that may engage PIPL thresholds, "important data" or HGR rules are recorded as named legal-review action items for qualified PRC and HK counsel.

Penetration Test Scope: Independent, Two-Pass, Rules-Bound

A penetration test is an authorised, controlled attempt to find and exploit weaknesses the way an attacker would. It is performed by a team independent of the GRC consultants who design the controls — so the people grading the work are not the people who did it.

In scope

  • External perimeter — all internet-facing IP addresses and domains (confirmed list, Week 2); email domain spoofing checks (SPF/DKIM/DMARC)
  • Microsoft 365 tenant configuration review — identity, sharing, conditional access, logging, against a recognised benchmark
  • Jianguoyun configuration review — admin console, sharing, permissions (subject to edition)
  • One internal network segment at the HK office — assumed-breach view of what a compromised laptop can reach
  • Optional phishing simulation — two-door below

Out of scope

  • Yonyou Cloud application-layer testing (SaaS vendor's responsibility; configuration review only)
  • Denial-of-service, social engineering beyond the phishing simulation, physical intrusion
  • Mainland internal networks and assets, unless PRC counsel confirms testing is legally and contractually permitted (Week 1–2)
  • Lab instrument networks and ELN/LIMS (Gate 3)

Signed rules of engagement before any testing · Pass 1 baseline Weeks 5–8 · remediation Weeks 9–18 (client + delivery team) · Pass 2 retest Weeks 19–22 · severity on CVSS-based scale mapped to P0/P1/P2 · critical findings to Sponsor within 24 hours.
